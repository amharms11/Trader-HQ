# Trade blue chips, cap the memes, respect fees

A beginner's crypto bot on Alpaca can trade about 25 coins, but it should spend most of its time on Bitcoin and Ether and treat meme coins as a small, tightly capped side bet. Alpaca lists blue chips (BTC, ETH, SOL, XRP, LTC, BCH, LINK, AVAX, AAVE, ADA) and the meme coins DOGE, SHIB, PEPE and TRUMP, with BONK and WIF added in 2026. Every coin trades against the US dollar, around the clock, long-only, with no shorting and no margin. The biggest single fact for a small bot is cost. At the starting fee tier, buying and selling with market orders costs about **0.50% per round trip before the spread**, so tiny "scalp" targets of 0.3–0.5% lose money by design. The market never closes, but its quality does change: liquidity is deepest in European and US weekday hours, the most volatile hour is the US stock market open, and weekends carry only about 13% of Bitcoin volume. Meme coins swing two to three times harder than Bitcoin, have fallen 85–98% from their peaks, and crash together. The social channels that drive them (X, Telegram, Reddit) are heavily polluted by paid promoters and bots. Sentiment tools such as LunarCrush and CoinGecko trending show only a small, short-lived predictive edge that fees can easily erase. The sensible plan is to paper trade BTC and ETH during weekday daytime hours with stops and targets sized to volatility, then add a capped meme sleeve only after the core strategy proves itself. A word of honesty up front: the evidence is weak that any simple retail intraday strategy beats costs. Most retail crypto traders lose money, so the bot should be judged against simply buying and holding Bitcoin.

A note on sources: the researchers could not open Alpaca's own website directly, so Alpaca figures come from search summaries of Alpaca pages. The bot should confirm the coin list, fees and order sizes from the live API before going live.

## Alpaca lists about 25 coins, six of them meme coins

Alpaca's support page on supported pairs lists these USD pairs: AAVE, AVAX, BCH, BTC, DOGE, ETH, LINK, LTC, PEPE, SHIB, SOL, TRUMP, XRP and the stablecoins USDC and USDT ([Alpaca Support](https://alpaca.markets/support/what-are-the-supported-coins-pairs)). In mid-May 2026 Alpaca announced 11 more coins, including **Cardano (ADA), Arbitrum (ARB), BONK, Filecoin (FIL), Hyperliquid (HYPE), Lido (LDO), Ondo (ONDO), POL, Render (RENDER) and dogwifhat (WIF)**. All of them trade 24/7 with market, limit and stop-limit orders and allow fractional sizes ([Alpaca Blog](https://alpaca.markets/blog/alpaca-adds-new-crypto-coins-to-offering/); [Crowdfund Insider](https://www.crowdfundinsider.com/2026/05/280485-alpaca-expands-crypto-portfolio-with-cardano-ondo-arbitrum-other-digital-assets/)). Several coins from Alpaca's older list, including DOT, UNI, MKR and CRV, no longer appear in the 2026 pair table ([older Alpaca list](https://alpaca.markets/support/what-cryptocurrencies-does-alpaca-currently-support)). The researchers found no formal delisting notice, which is one more reason to read the live list from the API (`/v2/assets?asset_class=crypto`) every time the bot starts rather than hard-coding it.

In practice, the tradable universe splits into three groups. The blue chips are BTC, ETH, SOL, XRP, LTC, BCH, LINK, AVAX, AAVE and ADA. The mid-cap and DeFi names are ARB, FIL, HYPE, LDO, ONDO, POL and RENDER. The meme coins are DOGE, SHIB, PEPE, TRUMP, BONK and WIF. Newer coins and meme coins appear to trade **only against USD**, so a dollar-funded bot should stick to `COIN/USD` pairs. Because Alpaca only lists established, exchange-traded meme coins, the bot is shielded from the worst meme coin danger, which is brand-new tokens whose creators drain the money. That protection does not cover price crashes.

Two account details matter before going live. Crypto **cannot be sold short** and is **not marginable**, so the bot can only make money when prices rise and can only spend cash it actually holds ([Alpaca Docs](https://alpaca.markets/docs/crypto-trading); [Alpaca Docs: Spot Trading](https://docs.alpaca.markets/us/docs/crypto-trading)). Live crypto access has also depended on your state. Alpaca's 2022 launch covered 49 states and excluded New York ([BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20220308005090/en/Alpaca-Crypto-API-Now-Offers-Over-20-New-Coins-and-Expands-Access-to-49-US-States)), and forum users report trading crypto on paper but not live because of where they live ([Alpaca Forum](https://forum.alpaca.markets/t/enabling-crypto-trading/7035)). Check the live account's crypto status before you assume paper results will carry over.

## Fees of 0.5% per round trip set the floor for every target

Alpaca charges a percentage fee on each crypto trade, based on your crypto trading volume over the past 30 days. A new account sits in Tier 1 (under $100,000 of monthly volume) and pays **0.15% as a "maker"**, meaning a limit order that waits on the order book, and **0.25% as a "taker"**, meaning a market order or a limit order that fills immediately. Tier 2 starts at $100,000 of monthly volume and pays 0.12% and 0.22% ([Alpaca Docs: Crypto Fees](https://docs.alpaca.markets/us/docs/crypto-fees)). Only crypto volume counts toward the tier, and the schedule has been unchanged since March 2023 ([Alpaca Support FAQ](https://alpaca.markets/support/crypto-maker-taker-gmt-faq)).

Here is what that means in plain numbers. Buy and sell with market orders and you pay **0.50% of the trade value** in fees. Buy and sell with resting limit orders that both fill as maker and you pay 0.30%. Then add the spread, the gap between the best buying and selling prices. The researchers found no measured 2026 Alpaca spreads. Older forum threads from 2021–2022 complained of Bitcoin spreads up to 2.5% and of spreads high enough to make "most strategies" unworkable ([Alpaca Forum](https://forum.alpaca.markets/t/high-crypto-spread/7094); [Alpaca Forum](https://forum.alpaca.markets/t/extremely-high-spreads-in-crypto/8321)). Those threads are old, but they show why the bot must record real bid and ask prices for every coin before you trust any profit target. The total cost of a round trip is therefore roughly **0.5–0.8% for BTC and ETH, and more for meme coins**. A target below about 1% leaves almost nothing after costs. The academic evidence agrees: a 2026 study found that a Bitcoin momentum strategy "loses heavily after transaction costs because it trades too often" ([arXiv 2606.00060](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.00060v1)).

The order types add a second constraint. Crypto on Alpaca supports market, limit and stop-limit orders, with "good till canceled" (`gtc`) or "immediate or cancel" (`ioc`) timing ([Alpaca Docs: Crypto Orders](https://docs.alpaca.markets/us/docs/crypto-orders)). Alpaca staff have said **bracket orders do not work for crypto**, and users report the related one-triggers-other orders failing too ([Alpaca Forum](https://forum.alpaca.markets/t/is-it-possible-to-make-a-bracket-order-for-crypto/8085); [Alpaca Forum](https://forum.alpaca.markets/t/crypto-order-with-order-class/11596)). Trailing stops are not supported either. So the bot has to run its own exits: after a buy fills, it places a take-profit limit sell, watches the price for the stop, and cancels the leftover order once one side fires. A stop-limit can also fail to fill in a fast crash, because the price jumps straight past the limit. For volatile coins, a stop that your code watches and closes with a market sell is safer.

The minimum order is about **$10** on USD pairs, and fractional and dollar-amount ("notional") orders are allowed ([Alpaca Docs: Crypto Orders](https://docs.alpaca.markets/us/docs/crypto-orders)). Each coin has its own `min_trade_increment` and `price_increment`, and orders that ignore them get rejected ([Alpaca Docs: Crypto Assets](https://docs.alpaca.markets/docs/crypto-assets)).

Paper trading fills orders against real-time quotes, but it does not model market impact, delay or your place in the order queue ([Alpaca Docs: Paper Trading](https://docs.alpaca.markets/docs/paper-trading)). Forum users call paper fills "notoriously generous" ([Alpaca Forum](https://forum.alpaca.markets/t/slippage-paper-trading-vs-real-trading/2801)). The researchers also could not confirm whether paper crypto trades are charged fees. Assume paper profits are too rosy: subtract the fees yourself, plus about 0.05% of extra slippage per side for BTC and ETH and 0.2–0.5% per side for meme coins. Those slippage numbers are judgment calls, not measured figures. Limit-order strategies look especially good on paper, because paper fills them as soon as the price touches.

On taxes, every trade the bot makes is a taxable short-term gain or loss. Brokers report gross proceeds on Form 1099-DA for sales from 2025 onward, and cost basis for coins bought from January 2026 onward ([Harness](https://www.harness.co/articles/new-1099-da-broker-reporting-rules-for-2026-what-crypto-investors-need-to-know)). The wash-sale rule still does not apply to crypto held directly. Bills to change that, most recently the 2026 PARITY Act, had not passed as of the research ([TurboTax](https://ttlc.intuit.com/turbotax-support/en-us/help-article/cryptocurrency/wash-sale-rule-cryptocurrency/L1d6BuQpH_US_en_US); [dTax](https://getdtax.com/blog/us-crypto-tax-legislation-debate-2026)). Keep the bot's own trade log, fees included, because a busy bot can generate thousands of tax line items.

## The 24/7 market runs on Wall Street hours

Crypto never closes, but Bitcoin now moves mostly when US traders are awake. A study of nine years of hourly Kraken data found that **the nine hours from 13:00 to 21:59 UTC produced 50.6% of Bitcoin's daily price movement in 2022–2025**, up from 38.4% in 2016–2018. The single most volatile hour lines up with the 9:30 a.m. New York stock market open, so it shifts by one hour when US clocks change ([CryptoSlate](https://cryptoslate.com/?p=561774)). The same "Wall Street time" pattern shows up in ETH, XRP and SOL ([CryptoSlate](https://cryptoslate.com/crypto-never-closes-but-bitcoin-ethereum-xrp-and-solana-now-move-on-wall-street-time/)). US daylight saving time ended on November 1, 2026, so the open is now **14:30 UTC**. It returns to 13:30 UTC in March.

Liquidity, meaning how much you can buy or sell without moving the price, follows a different rhythm. Amberdata found that Bitcoin's order books are **deepest around 11:00 UTC**, when Asian, European and US traders overlap, and **about 42% thinner by 21:00 UTC**. The thinnest stretch is the handover from the US afternoon into the Asian open ([Amberdata](https://blog.amberdata.io/the-rhythm-of-liquidity-temporal-patterns-in-market-depth)). Weekends are thinner still. Kaiko measured that only **about 13% of Bitcoin volume traded on weekends in early 2024**, and just 11% on US exchanges ([CoinDesk/Kaiko](https://www.coindesk.com/video/only-13percent-of-bitcoin-transactions-in-2024-were-executed-over-the-weekend-kaiko/)). About 47% of volume now happens during US weekday sessions since the Bitcoin ETFs launched ([DailyCoin](https://dailycoin.com/bitcoins-weekend-gap-etfs-shift-liquidity-to-u-s-hours/)). Sources disagree on whether weekend liquidity recovered somewhat in 2025 or kept shrinking ([Bitcoin.com News](https://news.bitcoin.com/report-sundays-see-bitcoin-trading-spike-linked-to-institutional-market-hours); [CryptoSlate](https://cryptoslate.com/?p=528752)). Either way, weekends are clearly the riskier period.

Thin markets turn ordinary drops into wipeouts. In the October 10, 2025 crash, about $19 billion of leveraged positions were wiped out in a day. Smaller coins fell **33% in 25 minutes**, and some exchanges' buy-side order books "went nearly blank" ([CryptoSlate](https://cryptoslate.com/?p=502753); [Coin Metrics](https://coinmetrics.io/?p=27655)). In a moment like that, a stop-loss gets filled far below its trigger price, or not at all.

The practical takeaway for a small bot is to **trade when order books are deep, not when they are exciting**. The best window for entries is weekdays from about 07:00 to 20:00 UTC. The US open, 14:30–16:00 UTC in winter, is the best time for breakout entries but also the most likely to whipsaw you. The bot should stop opening new trades in the evening and overnight, and stay flat through the weekend. No hour-by-hour data exists for DOGE, SHIB, PEPE, BONK or WIF. Their order books are thinner at every hour, so the same rules should apply to them even more strictly.

## Meme coins swing three times harder and correlate in crashes

The researchers found no clean 2025–2026 volatility table, so these figures are estimates. One informal 2019–2024 analysis put yearly volatility at roughly **70% for BTC, 80% for ETH, 100% for SOL, 150% for DOGE, 180% for SHIB, and 200% or more for smaller meme coins** ([Medium](https://medium.com/@gwrx2005/price-correlation-between-major-cryptocurrencies-and-memecoins-2019-2024-6ce899224366)). Converted to a typical day, that is about a 3–5% daily price range for BTC, 4–6% for ETH, 5–8% for SOL, 7–12% for DOGE, and **10–20% for SHIB, PEPE, BONK and WIF**. These ranges are rough conversions, so the bot should measure them live using ATR (average true range, a standard measure of how far a price typically moves in a period). The extreme days are what really separate the two groups. A 2025 study recorded SHIB volatility of 63% on a day when ETH's was 5.6%, and DOGE reaching 29% in November 2024 ([arXiv 2512.00377](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.00377v1)). Meme volatility also changes with market mood: in a quiet stretch of 2023, DOGE was briefly calmer than Bitcoin ([FXStreet](https://www.fxstreet.com/amp/cryptocurrencies/news/dogecoin-beats-bitcoin-in-price-stability-amid-crypto-trading-lull-202309221251)).

The longer-term picture is worse. **WIF sits about 96% below its March 2024 peak, PEPE about 86%, and BONK 87–95%**. These figures come from promotional press releases citing CoinMarketCap data, so check them before relying on them ([openPR](https://www.openpr.com/news/4663356/next-pepe-coin-sec-clears-yes-or-no-bets-wif-sits-96-down); [openPR](https://www.openpr.com/news/4619247/bonk-price-prediction-drops-95-from-its-peak-and-pepeto-is)). In 2025, DOGE was down about 20% for the year by October, BONK about 33%, and SHIB, PEPE and TRUMP did worse, while Bitcoin held up better ([Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/bitcoin-crushing-memecoins-2025-can-doge-trump-rebound)). Meme coins also fall together, often by double digits on the same day ([CryptoPotato](https://cryptopotato.com/meme-coin-bloodbath-as-pepe-bonk-wif-pnut-popcat-dump-by-double-digits/)). Holding three meme coins is therefore close to holding one meme coin three times over.

TRUMP deserves a specific warning. Only about **20% of its supply was tradable at launch**. The remaining 80% sits with insiders and unlocks over three years ([Blockworks](https://blockworks.co/news/trump-coin-unlock-token-holdings)). Each scheduled unlock adds supply that can push the price down. The coin fell about 95% from its January 2025 peak of about $75. Nansen and the New York Times estimated about **989,000 wallets with combined losses of $3.81 billion** by mid-2025, while 58 wallets made more than $10 million each ([KuCoin/Nansen-NYT](https://www.kucoin.com/news/flash/trump-memecoin-loses-97-as-investors-lose-3-8b-trump-earns-1-4b); [Statista/Chainalysis](https://de.statista.com/infografik/amp/34489/tageskurs-des-official-trump-memecoins)).

Beyond what Alpaca lists, the wider meme coin market is mostly fraud. Solidus Labs flagged **98.6% of more than 7 million pump.fun tokens** as rug pulls or pump-and-dumps ([CoinDesk](https://hotfix.coindesk.com/business/2025/05/07/98-of-tokens-on-pump-fun-have-been-rug-pulls-or-an-act-of-fraud-new-report-says)). Pump.fun disputes those findings. Chainalysis found that about 89% of trading pools tied to suspected pump-and-dumps were drained by their own creators ([CryptoRank](https://cryptorank.io/news/feed/6a65e-89-of-dex-pools-exploited-by-creators-in-pump-and-dump-schemes-chainalysis)). US buyers also have little legal protection. In February 2025, SEC staff said meme coins are not securities, which leaves victims with fraud enforcement and private lawsuits ([Winston & Strawn](https://www.winston.com/en/blogs-and-podcasts/non-fungible-insights-blockchain-decrypted/sec-staff-under-new-administration-issues-statement-declaring-that-meme-coins-are-not-securities); [Bloomberg Law](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/securities-law/memecoin-suits-test-trumps-sec-on-pivot-in-crypto-enforcement)).

## Realistic strategies: daytime momentum on blue chips, tiny bets on memes

The research does not support any proven, easy edge. The best academic result is that **Bitcoin's first half-hour of the trading "day" predicts its last half-hour** ([University of Reading](https://centaur.reading.ac.uk/100181)). But that paper's break-even trading cost was only 0.29–0.96% per trade, and it assumed 10-to-1 leverage ([accepted manuscript](https://centaur.reading.ac.uk/100181/3/21Sep2021Bitcoin%20Intraday%20Time-Series%20Momentum.R2.pdf)). A cash-only bot paying 0.5% or more per round trip would see most of that edge eaten. Strategies that trade every hour lose heavily after costs ([arXiv 2606.00060](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.00060v1)). The researchers found no credible after-fee evidence for mean reversion, VWAP or opening-range strategies on spot crypto. Grid trading, which places a ladder of small buy and sell orders, has only anecdotes, and they are bad. One grid bot won 82% of its trades and still lost money, because **666 small wins (+$1,917) were outweighed by 28 stop-losses (−$3,601)** ([DEV Community](https://dev.to/joe_hans_6c082d6c1caf189f/i-ran-a-gateio-btc-grid-bot-for-6-months-real-returns-and-3-fatal-mistakes-2i1p)). Another grid backtest made 197 USDT from the grid itself while losing 33% overall ([Binance Square](https://www.binance.com/square/hashtag/gridbot)). On a falling meme coin, a grid bot simply keeps buying more of something that is going down.

For **blue chips**, the most defensible design is long-only trend-following breakouts. Trade only BTC and ETH, possibly SOL, and only when the bigger trend is up, for example when price is above its daily moving average. That filter matters because a spot bot cannot profit from falling prices. Enter on breakouts in the US-open window and exit at a profit target, at a trailing stop based on ATR, or at a fixed time before the evening liquidity drop. Make fewer, larger trades. This is a hypothesis to test in paper trading with Alpaca's real fees, not a proven strategy.

For **meme coins**, the honest answer is that no reliable day-trading edge was found. Their larger swings make room for targets well above the fee floor, but their thin order books, wider spreads, crash-together behavior and sudden 30% drops mean stops slip badly. The only reasonable approach is the same trend-filtered momentum logic, with much smaller positions, wider stops, and a hard limit on total meme exposure.

Keep expectations in check. A Bank for International Settlements study estimated that **73–81% of retail investors lost money on their first Bitcoin investments** ([Bloomberg Tax](https://news.bloombergtax.com/crypto/about-75-of-retail-buyers-of-bitcoin-lost-money-bis-study-says)). Studies of stock and futures day traders in Taiwan and Brazil found that over 97% of persistent day traders lost money, and fewer than 1% were reliably profitable ([Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/markets/day-trading-bitcoin-why-95-of-traders-lose-money-and-fail)). The bot should beat buy-and-hold BTC in paper trading, after the fee and slippage haircut, before any real money goes in.

## Social buzz is mostly paid, and sentiment trails the price

Meme coin hype starts on X, Telegram, Discord and launchpads, and much of it is bought. In 2025, the on-chain investigator ZachXBT linked payments to more than 160 influencers who took paid promotion deals. **Fewer than 5 labeled their posts as ads** ([The Block](https://www.theblock.co/post/368956/zachxbt-says-over-100-crypto-influencers-accepted-promo-deals-without-disclosing-paid-ads)). Paid posts reportedly cost $1,500–$60,000 each, and gray-market operations run dozens of farmed accounts that post nearly 300 tweets a day ([ChainCatcher](https://www.chaincatcher.com/en/article/2202681)). A study of 1,567 meme tokens found that **76% of X crypto influencers had promoted a meme coin that went to zero**, and only 1% had promoted one that rose tenfold ([CryptoRank/Coinwire](https://cryptorank.io/news/feed/44bd2-coinwire-report-on-memecoin-influencers)). On Telegram, researchers have documented more than 1,000 confirmed pumps by 20 organized groups ([arXiv 2105.00733](https://arxiv.org/html/2105.00733v2)). Public pump groups often repost signals from private ones, so insiders buy first ([arXiv 2605.09431](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.09431)). Reddit has no comparable recent study. One thesis found that Reddit sentiment did not predict Bitcoin returns; instead, **price moves drove Reddit sentiment** ([CEU thesis](https://www.etd.ceu.edu/2023/zhumagaziyev_sh.pdf)). The pattern is consistent across these sources: by the time a coin is trending, the early buyers are the ones selling, and a bot that buys on buzz becomes their exit.

Packaged sentiment data is cheap but weak. **CoinGecko's trending endpoint is free** and simply shows the most-searched coins ([CoinGecko docs](https://docs.coingecko.com/docs/keyless-public-api.md)). **LunarCrush** sells social and sentiment scores, including its "Galaxy Score", at $90 a month, or $300 a month for full API access ([LunarCrush](https://lunarcrush.com/support/how-much-does-lunarcrush-cost)). Santiment starts at about $49 a month ([CostBench](https://costbench.com/software/onchain-analytics/santiment/)). The research found no peer-reviewed test of LunarCrush or Santiment as trading signals. The best academic estimate is that a rise in social sentiment predicts about **0.25% higher return the next day** ([Springer, Electronic Markets](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s12525-025-00815-6)). That is smaller than a single Alpaca round trip. The Crypto Fear & Greed Index has some published support as a predictor of BTC and ETH returns ([Emerald/RBF](https://www.emerald.com/insight/content/doi/10.1108/RBF-08-2023-0224/full/html)), but practitioners report that "buy the fear" does not work over short horizons ([MEXC](https://www.mexc.com/news/1197268)).

If the bot uses AI, there is one more danger. Researchers showed that messages posted on X or Discord could plant false "memories" in an AI crypto agent and make it **send funds to attackers**, and that prompt-based defenses failed ([arXiv 2503.16248](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.16248v1)). Use sentiment only as numbers, never as raw post text. Use those numbers to shrink or pause trading, not to trigger buys. Never let social text reach the part of the bot that places orders.

## Recommended bot settings

These settings turn the evidence above into starting values. The rules about fees, trading hours and avoiding social text come directly from the cited sources. The specific numbers for risk, caps and spread filters are judgment calls, chosen to keep a beginner safe while paper trading proves or disproves the strategy. Use UTC for every time setting. The windows below assume US standard time, which runs until March 2027, so move them one hour earlier during daylight saving time.

| Setting | Recommended value |
|---|---|
| Coin universe (read from API at startup) | `COIN/USD` pairs only; refuse any symbol not on this allowlist |
| Core coins (phase 1, paper) | BTC/USD, ETH/USD |
| Secondary coins (phase 2) | SOL/USD; optionally XRP, LINK, AVAX, LTC after separate paper testing |
| Meme coins (phase 3, only after core is profitable on paper for 2–3 months) | DOGE/USD first; SHIB/USD and PEPE/USD as optional extras |
| Meme coins to exclude | TRUMP (insider unlock overhang), BONK and WIF (newest listings, ~87–96% drawdowns); never trade new launches |
| Entry window | Weekdays 07:00–20:00 UTC; breakout entries focused on 14:30–16:30 UTC (US open) |
| No-new-entry window | 21:00–07:00 UTC daily |
| Weekend | Flat from Friday 20:00 UTC to Sunday 23:00 UTC; close positions before the weekend |
| Trend filter | Long only when price is above its 1-day 20–50 period moving average |
| Order type | Limit entries where possible (0.15% maker); market exits allowed for stops |
| Assumed round-trip cost in backtests and paper | BTC/ETH 0.6%; SOL 0.7%; meme coins 1.0–1.5% (fees plus slippage) |
| Spread filter (skip trade if spread is wider) | BTC/ETH 0.10%; SOL and other blue chips 0.20%; meme coins 0.40% |
| Risk per trade (entry minus stop, as % of equity) | Blue chips 0.5–1.0%; meme coins 0.25% |
| Max single position | 25% of equity for BTC/ETH; 10% for others; 5% per meme coin |
| Stop-loss | 1.5–2.5 × 1-hour ATR; roughly 1–2% for BTC, 1.5–3% for ETH/SOL, 4–8% for meme coins |
| Take-profit | At least 2× the stop distance and at least 1.5% (blue chips) or 8% (meme coins); or trail with 2 × ATR |
| Time exit | Close any open trade by 20:30 UTC |
| Max open positions | 3 total, of which at most 1 meme coin |
| Total meme exposure cap | 10% of the crypto account (never more than 15%) |
| Max round trips per day | 3–6 |
| Daily loss limit | −2% of equity: stop until the next UTC day |
| Weekly loss limit | −5% of equity: stop until next week and review |
| Circuit breaker | Price moves more than 3 × ATR within minutes, quote data is stale, or spread exceeds the filter: cancel orders and pause |
| Sentiment use | Optional numeric filter only (e.g. halve size or pause memes when Fear & Greed is "Extreme Greed" or social volume spikes); never a buy signal; never raw post text |
| API key permissions | Trading only; no transfers or withdrawals |
| Go-live test | After fees and slippage haircut, beat buy-and-hold BTC over 2–3 months of paper trading; go live at 10–25% of the intended size |

## Conclusion

The common beginner picture of crypto day trading, with a bot scalping meme coins around the clock and riding social media buzz, is close to the opposite of what this evidence supports. On Alpaca, the binding limit is not access to coins or hours. It is a **0.5% cost floor** on every round trip, set against a market whose deep, tradable hours look much like the stock market's. That pushes a sensible bot toward fewer, larger, daytime trades on Bitcoin and Ether, where costs are a small share of the typical move and order books can absorb a stop-loss. Meme coins do not offer a better opportunity, just a bigger bet: their larger swings come with fatter crash risk, prices that fall together, and a hype machine designed to make late buyers the exit. That is why the meme sleeve should be capped, not optimized.

The most valuable thing the bot can produce in its first months is not profit but measurement. It should record its real Alpaca spreads, its real slippage compared with paper fills, and its real ATR per coin, because the most important numbers for this decision were exactly the ones the research could not confirm. If the strategy cannot beat simply holding Bitcoin after those measured costs, the right move is to stop day trading, not to add meme coins.
