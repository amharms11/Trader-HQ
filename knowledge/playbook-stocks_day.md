# Stock day trading

- The first 15–30 minutes after the 9:30 ET open have the widest spreads and the most noise. Prefer waiting for the opening range to form before entering; avoid market orders right at the open.
- Midday (about 11:30–14:00 ET) is usually quiet with weak follow-through. Be extra selective.
- The last hour has strong volume and the closing auction is the most liquid time to exit. Close day trades before the bell.
- Intraday momentum is modest: the first half hour's direction weakly predicts the last half hour's (small effect, before costs). Don't rely on it alone.
- Best-supported intraday context: trade "stocks in play" (unusually high relative volume, news-driven activity), in the direction of the day's trend, with VWAP as the dividing line (above VWAP favors longs). The opening-range-breakout and VWAP results are working papers, not proven, so keep size small.
- Enter on a confirmed setup (a candle close, not mid-candle), near a clear level, with a stop beyond that level. Skip if the stop must be so wide that the risk limit forces a tiny position.
- Avoid entries right before scheduled events: Fed announcements (2:00 PM ET on FOMC days), earnings, and major economic releases at 8:30 or 10:00 ET.
- Most stock gains historically happen overnight, not intraday. Day trading gives that up, so each day trade needs a real reason.
- Stops help most when prices are trending; in choppy, rangebound days, stand aside rather than getting stopped out repeatedly.
- After 2 losing trades in a row, be much pickier for the rest of the day (no revenge trading).

## Trading the live chart (1- and 5-minute)
- Read in this order: the stock's daily trend and key levels, then the 5-minute chart for structure (opening range, VWAP, trend), then the 1-minute chart only to time the entry. A setup on the 1-minute chart that fights the 5-minute trend is usually a trap.
- The setups with at least some published, rule-based testing all enter fast and exit slowly: the 5-minute opening range breakout on stocks in play (direction of the first 5-minute candle, stop about 10% of the daily ATR beyond the range, hold toward the close), VWAP trend (long only while price holds above VWAP), and the first-half-hour-predicts-last-half-hour effect on SPY. Prefer them over seconds-long scalps.
- Bull flags, micro pullbacks, gap-and-go, ABCD and red-to-green have rules but no independent evidence. Keep size small on them.
- "Stocks in play" means unusually high relative volume (2x+ normal) on real news, price above $5, average volume over 1M shares, and a tight spread. Avoid cheap low-float stocks: halts, wide spreads and pump-and-dumps make stops unreliable.
- Costs decide fast trades. A typical retail round trip costs 0.07–0.46% depending on the broker before any slippage. Break-even win rate = (stop + cost) / (target + stop). A 0.2% target can need about a 75% win rate. Skip targets smaller than about 3x the spread plus costs.
- Stops become market orders when hit, and trading halts (LULD pauses) can gap price far past them. Size for the gap, not just the stop.
- The free IEX price feed covers only about 2% of volume, so VWAP and volume readings from it are rough. Treat volume signals cautiously unless the full (SIP) feed is on.
- A plan made from 5-minute data goes stale fast: only act on a setup if price is still near the planned entry. Never chase a candle that has already run.
