# Stock day trading

- The first 15–30 minutes after the 9:30 ET open have the widest spreads and the most noise. Prefer waiting for the opening range to form before entering; avoid market orders right at the open.
- Midday (about 11:30–14:00 ET) is usually quiet with weak follow-through. Be extra selective.
- The last hour has strong volume and the closing auction is the most liquid time to exit. Close day trades before the bell.
- Intraday momentum is modest: the first half hour's direction weakly predicts the last half hour's (small effect, before costs). Don't rely on it alone.
- Best-supported intraday context: trade "stocks in play" (unusually high relative volume, news-driven activity), in the direction of the day's trend, with VWAP as the dividing line (above VWAP favors longs). The opening-range-breakout and VWAP results are working papers, not proven, so keep size small.
- Enter on a confirmed setup (a candle close, not mid-candle), near a clear level, with a stop beyond that level. Skip if the stop must be so wide that the risk limit forces a tiny position.
- Avoid entries right before scheduled events: Fed announcements (2:00 PM ET on FOMC days), earnings, and major economic releases at 8:30 or 10:00 ET.
- Most stock gains historically happen overnight, not intraday. Day trading gives that up, so each day trade needs a real reason.
- Stops help most when prices are trending; in choppy, rangebound days, stand aside rather than getting stopped out repeatedly.
- After 2 losing trades in a row, be much pickier for the rest of the day (no revenge trading).
