# General rules (all bots)

- Doing nothing is a good answer. Most checks should end with no trade. Fewer, better trades beat many small ones: costs and spreads eat frequent trading (Barber & Odean: the most active households earned 11.4%/yr vs 17.9% for the market).
- Fewer than 1% of day traders are reliably profitable after costs (Taiwan, 15 years of data). 97% of persistent Brazilian day traders lost money. Act like the odds are against short-term trades, because they are.
- Risk a small, fixed slice per trade (about 0.5–1% of the account between entry and stop). Size from the stop distance, not from confidence.
- Every entry needs a planned exit: a stop-loss and a target set before buying. Targets should be at least about 1.5–2x the stop distance.
- Don't sell winners early and hold losers hoping they come back (the disposition effect is well documented and costly). Let the stop and target do the work.
- Chart patterns and indicators carry little reliable edge on their own. Famous candle patterns test close to a coin flip, and old indicator rules faded once costs and data-snooping were counted. Use them only as context: trend direction, where price sits in its range, and volume.
- Location matters more than the pattern: reversal signals mean more near a 52-week high or low or a clear support/resistance level than in the middle of the range.
- Volume confirms. A move on strong relative volume is more trustworthy than one on thin volume.
- ATR (average true range) is a sensible yardstick for stop distance: about 1.5–2.5x ATR on the timeframe being traded.
- Never chase a big move that already happened. Never trade on rumors, hype or "it's trending".
- Judge results over many trades (hundreds for small edges), after costs, against simply holding SPY.
- The owner's plan: most money stays in a cheap index-fund core; the bots are a small, capped "satellite" that must beat simply holding SPY (or Bitcoin for crypto) after costs. Protecting capital matters more than catching every move.
