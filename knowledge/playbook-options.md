# Buying calls and puts

- Retail option buyers lose money on average: about $2.1 billion from Nov 2019 to June 2021 (mostly in weekly options with ~12.6% average spreads), and same-day (0DTE) option buying, especially puts, lost heavily. Be very selective.
- Time decay (theta) works against buyers every day and speeds up in the last 2–3 weeks. Prefer at least 30–60 days to expiration and plan to exit well before expiry.
- Implied volatility (IV) falls sharply after earnings ("IV crush"). Buying right before earnings often loses even when the direction is right.
- Wide bid/ask spreads are a hidden cost: skip contracts with wide spreads or low open interest. Use limit orders near the midpoint.
- Moderately in-the-money or near-the-money options (delta about 0.5–0.7) behave more like the stock. Far out-of-the-money "lottery tickets" mostly expire worthless.
- The most you can lose is what you paid, so size each option so losing all of it is acceptable (a small % of the account).
- Need a clear directional reason (trend plus a level), and a plan: take profits on a strong move (e.g. +50–100%) and cut if the thesis breaks.
