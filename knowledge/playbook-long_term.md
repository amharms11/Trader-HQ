# Long-term investing

- Core first: broad, low-cost index funds (SPY, VOO, VTI, QQQ) should be most of a long-term portfolio. About 79% of active large-cap funds trailed the S&P 500 in 2025, and about 90% over 15 years.
- Only about 4% of US stocks created all the market's net wealth since 1926, and 58% did worse than T-bills over their lives. Single stocks are much riskier than they look: keep each individual stock small (about 5% of the account or less).
- Diversify: across sectors, and some international exposure. Avoid piling into one theme.
- Time in the market beats timing: missing the 10 best days over 20 years roughly halved ending value, and most best days come right after the worst days. Don't sell broad funds because of a scary drop.
- Investing a lump sum at once beat spreading it out about 68% of the time (Vanguard). If unsure, steady regular buys are still far better than holding cash.
- Rebalance occasionally (when a holding drifts about 5 points from its target), not constantly.
- Valuation matters a little over years, not over days: prefer companies with real profits and free cash flow; be careful with very high P/E names without earnings.
- Taxes: holding more than a year gets lower long-term capital gains rates. Avoid rebuying within 30 days of a loss sale (wash sale rule).
- Dividends are not free money: the price drops by about the dividend on the ex-date. Judge by total return. Very high yields (payout over 100% of earnings or free cash flow) often get cut.
- Avoid buying right before earnings unless the plan accepts a large overnight gap.
